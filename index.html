The Context

Misinformation is now as critical as clinical care.

The Bundibugyo strain has no widely approved vaccine or specific therapeutic, so control depends on trust: early detection, safe care-seeking, contact tracing, and dignified burials. Fake cures, resistance to isolation, and rumors about treatment centers can undermine containment within hours.

  • 🗺️Unfolding across Ituri, Nord-Kivu & Sud-Kivu — ~5.9–7M people, nearly 1M displaced, high mining-and-trade mobility and persistent insecurity.
  • 🗣️Linguistically diverse: French, Swahili, Lingala, plus local languages like Lendu and Nyali. Biomedical vocabulary often doesn't survive translation.
  • ⚰️Funeral practices — body washing, mourning gatherings, burial location — are major transmission and trust issues. Messaging must adapt rituals, not just ban them.
  • 🌍Cross-border movement to Uganda, Rwanda & South Sudan complicates contact tracing and accelerates undetected transmission.

The Solution

A lightweight listening & response loop, built on tools the field already uses.

An open-source pipeline on top of KoboToolbox. It ingests community reports, uses an LLM to cluster scattered rumors into high-level thematic threats, and instantly generates targeted counter-messaging aligned with official public-health guidance — with a human always in the loop.

📥

Listen

Responders, community leaders & field workers log rumors via a simple KoboToolbox form — web or offline KoboCollect.

🧩

Cluster

An LLM groups dozens of localized reports — e.g. "poisoned food at the clinic" — into single semantic threat themes.

✍️

Draft

It auto-generates localized, de-escalating communication briefs optimized for radio, WhatsApp & CHV talking points.

✅

Verify

Trained moderators & RCCE focal points review every output. The AI drafts and analyzes — it never decides.

Where signals come from

Mapping the information landscape — and what's ethically scrapeable.

WhatsApp dominates communication across the region but is encrypted and private — so the system never monitors it directly. Instead it draws on opt-in tips, public posts, and radio. Radio still reaches an estimated 80%+ of Ugandans and broadcasts in local languages, making it more influential than social media in rural areas.

Hackathon MVP Scope

What we will build.

01 · Kobo Integration

Stand up a KoboToolbox deployment and design a simple form with text-entry fields for rapid rumor reporting.

02 · Backend Ingestion

A lightweight service (Node.js, Python, Go — any stack) retrieving incoming reports via the Kobo API or Webhooks.

03 · AI Processing

Connect the stream to an LLM (via OpenRouter or similar) to parse, categorize and synthesize text into narratives and counter-messages.

04 · Lightweight Dashboard

A simple open-source frontend (React, plain HTML, or Streamlit) showing active rumor trends and drafted counter-messaging.

Known Challenges

Honest about the hard parts.

🌐

Language coverage

Existing transcription tools handle Swahili and Luganda, but local dialects aren't supported — a local translator on the team is needed. We plan to validate in one mostly-Luganda-speaking community (the Baganda) first.

🔒

WhatsApp is a black box

Encryption means no scraping. Rumors spread fastest there in urban areas, so coverage depends on opt-in tips, a Business API number, and human monitors.

Practical priority order for language rollout:
English→ Luganda→ Regional languages via human monitors
